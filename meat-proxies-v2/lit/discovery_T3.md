**Gaps**

- **No quantitative overcorrection magnitudes.** SWAY (#1) reports that Claude models over-correct under anti-sycophancy instructions, but only qualitatively ("more likely to disagree"). I found no paper reporting a numeric tilt/lean shift like your F1 (+0.03 → −0.74) — quantified overcorrection appears unreported.
- **No studies of contrarian/"devil's advocate" persona prompts.** Searches for "be critical"/"be skeptical" system prompts and induced "disagreeableness" surfaced only neutral debiasing or role-reframing work (#6, #7); nobody seems to have characterized prompts that deliberately push models *against* the user.
- **Dose-response under debiasing prompts.** "Ask don't tell" (#2) finds user certainty modulates baseline sycophancy, but no study tests whether anti-sycophancy instructions interact with weak vs. strong user opinions — your F4 null result is untested territory.
- **No work on anti-sycophancy prompts in interpersonal/social conflict settings.** Both-sides validation (F6) is documented in ELEPHANT (#11) for older models, but no snippet reported testing debiasing instructions on interpersonal-conflict tasks, or measuring whether they reduce both-sides affirmation to ~0%.
- **No residual-framing measurement after debiasing.** Nothing found on whether anti-sycophancy prompting removes the *implicit* leakage of user opinion (framing/stance cues, your F2) once explicit agreement is suppressed — mitigation papers measure answer-level agreement only.
